Ransomware knocks out SoftBank-affiliated IDCF Cloud, attacker claims to have destroyed 41.5PB of backups across 495 clients

IDC Frontier's IDCF Cloud service suffered a ransomware attack beginning approximately 3:40am Japan time Oct 7, causing a large-scale outage across its East Japan Region 1 data center cluster and affecting 495 companies and local governments under contract. IDC Frontier told customers Oct 8 that data stored across four zones of that cluster may be difficult or impossible to retrieve, and the attacker claims to have destroyed 41.5 petabytes of backups — meaning recovery for affected organizations depends entirely on backups they kept outside IDCF Cloud themselves. IDC Frontier isolated the affected region and halted systems to contain secondary damage and potential data leakage. Durable catch: a 41.5PB backup-destruction claim against a cloud provider means every one of its 495 customers just had their disaster-recovery assumption invalidated at once — if your DR plan assumed your cloud provider's backups were a safety net independent of your own, this incident is the proof that assumption needed its own backup.

ARTEX — AI pentesting tool combined with multiple LLMs automates data-theft campaign against South Korean banks

CrowdStrike Intelligence disclosed a campaign active from late September to early October 2026 that combined ARTEX, an open-source Chinese-developed penetration-testing platform, with multiple large language models (DeepSeek v4.1-flash, GLM-5.3, and Grok 4.6) to automate reconnaissance, exploitation, and data exfiltration against South Korean financial organizations including Shinhan Bank, Kookmin Bank, Hana Bank, BNK Busan Bank, Yegaram Savings Bank, Hyundai Capital, and Welcome Savings Bank. CrowdStrike assesses with moderate confidence the operator is Chinese-speaking and financially motivated; open directories on attacker-controlled servers inadvertently exposed Claude Code session histories, ARTEX configuration files, and Claude memory files, letting researchers reconstruct the operator's prompt-by-prompt workflow. South Korea's Financial Services Commission and Financial Supervisory Service issued a public warning urging consumers to watch for phishing and loan scams stemming from the leaked data. Durable catch: the attacker's own exposed Claude Code session history is the actual intelligence goldmine here — reconstructing an adversary's prompt-by-prompt reasoning is a new category of attribution evidence that didn't exist before AI-agent-driven intrusions, and defenders should know to look for exposed AI tool session logs/config directories on any attacker infrastructure they gain visibility into.

Malicious npm release of 'tensorlake' extends the ChainDrop/Shai-Hulud worm into AI agent infrastructure

Socket flagged version 0.5.144 of the npm package 'tensorlake' (a TypeScript SDK for Tensorlake applications) shortly after its Oct 8 publication, finding it carried obfuscated malware consistent with prior ChainDrop/Shai-Hulud supply-chain compromises. The payload harvests and exfiltrates npm tokens, GitHub tokens, AWS credentials, Kubernetes credentials, SSH keys, cryptocurrency wallets, and — notably — configuration files for AI tools including Anthropic Claude, establishes persistence, and can execute remotely supplied code. This marks the campaign's extension from general developer-credential theft into AI-agent-specific infrastructure, since Claude and similar tool configs often hold API keys and session context with their own blast radius. Durable catch: a compromised dependency now specifically hunts for AI-tool configuration files alongside the usual cloud/SSH/crypto targets — any organization auditing a supply-chain compromise's blast radius needs to add 'what AI tool configs and API keys were present on affected machines' to the standard credential-rotation checklist, not just cloud and git credentials.

South Africa's air navigation provider finds ransomware-linked malware in OT network supporting aviation weather services

Air Traffic and Navigation Services (ATNS), which manages roughly 10% of the world's airspace and handles South Africa's air traffic control and aviation weather operations, discovered malware commonly associated with early-stage ransomware attacks inside its operational technology network supporting weather services. Preliminary findings suggested possible data exfiltration to external IP addresses in China; ATNS's technical team believes it contained the intrusion but is engaging external cyber-forensics firms, with the investigation also examining Port Elizabeth, East London, and Maputo International airports for a possible insider-assisted data theft angle. No disruption to actual air traffic or flights was reported, since the affected systems support weather services rather than live traffic control. Durable catch: this was caught at the ransomware-precursor stage, before encryption — the fact that early-stage malware in an OT network supporting weather services for 10% of the world's airspace was found before impact is itself the story worth noting, since most OT ransomware incidents in this digest are caught only after disruption.

CVE-2026-88779 — fourth NetScaler zero-day in two months knocks SAML-configured appliances offline in targeted attacks

Citrix published a bulletin Oct 3 for CVE-2026-88779 (CVSS 8.7), a memory-overflow denial-of-service flaw that lets an attacker with only network access — no valid account needed — overflow a NetScaler ADC/Gateway appliance's memory and force it offline, but only when the appliance is configured as a SAML service provider or identity provider for single sign-on. Citrix confirmed targeted-attack exploitation Oct 4; CISA added it to KEV Oct 5 with an Oct 7 federal deadline. This is the fourth distinct NetScaler CVE added to KEV within roughly two months, following CVE-2026-8452, CVE-2026-19490, and the CVE-2026-88771/88772 pair. Durable catch: four NetScaler KEV entries in two months is no longer a pattern of isolated bugs — any organization running NetScaler as a SAML SSO front end should treat the platform itself as a standing high-priority monitoring target rather than waiting for each individual CVE announcement.

European police dismantle KillSec ransomware infrastructure, arrest suspected 16-year-old lead operator

German-led international police action, 'Operation KillSwitch,' seized KillSec's data-leak site and five core servers Sep 30, blocking unauthorized access to more than 110 terabytes of stolen victim data and investigating a group linked to roughly 1,000 suspected cyberattacks worldwide. Spanish police arrested a 16-year-old Romanian national in Alicante as the suspected main operator; two additional suspects were provisionally arrested and eight properties searched across Greece, Romania, Spain, and the UK. The suspect faces potential extradition proceedings, including a reported US extradition question, alongside ongoing legal process in France regarding surrender of a minor under a European Arrest Warrant. Durable catch: 110TB of seized stolen data from a single takedown is a resource for organizations to check against — any entity that experienced an unexplained breach or extortion attempt in the group's operating window should inquire with the investigating agencies about whether their data appears in the seized cache, rather than assuming a takedown closes the matter for past victims.

ASOS breach traced to an employee tricked into handing over login credentials by an attacker posing as a trusted contact

ASOS confirmed a data breach after a group calling itself the Xuanye Group gained access to an employee's account by impersonating a trusted contact to obtain login credentials, then used that access to reach information on certain third-party platforms ASOS uses. ASOS says names, contact details, delivery and email addresses, phone numbers, and recent search history were accessed, but payment card data and account passwords were not affected; the company notified customers Oct 8 and has locked down the affected platforms while working with external experts, law enforcement, and regulators. Durable catch: the attacker didn't breach ASOS's own infrastructure at all — a single employee's credentials, obtained through impersonation, was sufficient to reach customer data on third-party platforms the company relies on, which is the actual control gap (third-party platform access tied to individual employee trust) rather than anything ASOS's own systems could have stopped.

Advantest confirms ransomware data theft 233 days after detection, citing cross-jurisdiction notification complexity

Advantest, which builds automatic test equipment chipmakers rely on to validate semiconductors, detected a ransomware attack Feb 15, 2026, and first acknowledged the incident publicly four days later while still investigating whether data had left the network. It took 233 days — until Oct 6 — to confirm and notify affected individuals that names, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, passport numbers, driver's license details, medical information, and financial records had been extracted from its servers. Advantest attributes the delay to reconstructing exactly what attackers accessed and matching recovered file listings against live record systems to identify affected individuals, compounded by operating across three continents where each jurisdiction carries its own notification rules; notifications went to over 500 California residents plus smaller numbers in Massachusetts and Vermont. Durable catch: Advantest's stated reason for the delay — reconstructing file-listing-to-individual mapping across jurisdictions — is a forensic capability gap worth testing in your own incident-response plan before an actual breach, since 'we don't yet know which specific individuals are in the stolen files' is a much harder problem than confirming data was taken at all.

Everest group claims flydubai breach, alleges theft of Boeing software source code and crew-training records

Hackmanac reported Oct 6 that the Everest cybercriminal group claims to have breached flydubai, alleging exfiltration of approximately 4.36GB across 16,517 files including flight-operations documents, crew-training records, personnel data, and proprietary Boeing software source code. flydubai has not publicly confirmed the claim or disclosed how the alleged intrusion occurred, and no sample files or additional technical detail have been independently verified as of this writing. Asahi Kasei Therapeutics reported Oct 6 that its website for healthcare professionals was hit by a cyberattack, potentially exposing personal information for up to 558,700 people through unauthorized access.
